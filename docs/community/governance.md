# Texture — Editorial Governance

Version 0.1 (draft). Who decides what goes in the books, and how.
Corporate governance (board, officers) is in
[../org/bylaws-outline.md](../org/bylaws-outline.md); this document covers
**content**.

---

## 1. Separation of Powers

| Body | Decides | Does NOT decide |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Board of Directors** | Budget, subjects/scope, hiring, legal, licensing policy | Content of any book |
| **Editorial Board** | Editorial standards, block-schema RFCs, Lead Editor appointments, disputes, credential criteria | Day-to-day book decisions |
| **Lead Editors** (per book) | Outline, merges, edition sign-off, contributor invitations | Standards that bind other books |
| **txt maintainers** | The tool | Content |

The corporate board funds and scopes; it does not edit. This firewall is
what lets Texture credibly claim content neutrality — donors and directors
have no channel to alter a book.

## 2. Editorial Board

- 7–11 members: practicing K-12 educators across grade bands, discipline
  scholars, a learning scientist, an accessibility expert.
- Appointed by the corporate board initially; self-renewing thereafter
  (board confirms, cannot nominate) — staggered 3-year terms.
- Public membership list with affiliations; annual COI disclosure
  ([COI policy §7](../org/conflict-of-interest-policy.md)).

## 3. Content Standards

1. **Accuracy:** scholarly consensus; contested scholarly questions are
   presented as contested; fringe positions are not balanced against
   consensus.
2. **Standards alignment:** K-12 books map to declared curriculum
   standards ([format §6](../txt/format.md)); alignment claims are
   verifiable in source.
3. **Neutrality:** curriculum topics are covered per the discipline's
   scholarly standards, not adjusted for political pressure. The Editorial
   Board is the sole authority on content disputes and publishes its
   reasoning.
4. **Age appropriateness:** pedagogy review is a hard gate (see
   [collaborators.md](collaborators.md)).
5. **Evidence-based pedagogy:** learning design choices (retrieval practice,
   worked examples, spacing) follow the learning-sciences evidence base;
   the Editorial Board maintains the house pedagogy guide.

## 4. Decision Processes

- **Routine:** Lead Editor decides; anything mergeable is theirs.
- **Cross-book standards** (style guide changes, block-schema RFCs, credential
  criteria): written RFC → open comment (contributors) → Editorial Board
  vote (majority). RFCs and votes are public.
- **Content disputes / challenges** (including external complaints about
  book content): written challenge → assigned panel of 3 Editorial Board
  members (COI-screened) → published written decision. Decisions cite
  sources. There is no private channel to change a book.
- **Emergency errata** (safety-relevant errors, e.g., a lab procedure):
  Lead Editor may hotfix and tag immediately; retroactive review within
  7 days.

## 5. Forks & Adaptations

CC BY 4.0 means anyone can adapt. Governance position:

- Forks are legitimate and welcome (state-specific adaptations,
  translations, classroom remixes).
- Forks may not use Texture trademarks or imply Texture editorial approval
  (see [brand guide §9](../brand/brand-guide.md)).
- High-quality adaptations can apply for **upstream adoption** or
  **"Texture Reviewed"** status through the normal review pipeline.

## 6. Transparency Commitments

- All review discussion on published books is public (reviewer identities
  per the credit policy).
- Editorial Board minutes published within 30 days (legal/personnel
  matters redacted).
- Annual editorial report: errata counts and closure times, review
  throughput, coverage maps, challenge decisions.
