# Texture — FAQ

Working answers for the website, press, and funder conversations. Keep in
sync with [MISSION.md](../../MISSION.md) and [licensing](../licensing.md).

---

**Is it really free? What's the catch?**
Digital editions are free for everyone, forever, with no accounts, no ads,
and no tracking. Print editions are sold at the lowest sustainable cost.
There is no catch; we're a nonprofit and the books are openly licensed
(CC BY 4.0), so even if Texture disappeared, the books remain free.

**Who writes the books?**
Credentialed educators and subject-matter experts, working in the open on
the Texture platform. Every book has a Lead Editor; every change passes
subject-matter review, pedagogy review, and copy editing before it's
published. Contributor credentials and the full review history are public.

**How is this different from Wikipedia?**
Openness at the front door, a publisher's editorial gate before anything
ships. Anyone can *propose* a change; only reviewed, credentialed-approved
changes reach students. Every published edition is signed by its editors.

**How is this different from OpenStax or other OER?**
We admire OpenStax and intend to interoperate, not compete — their college
titles proved open textbooks work at scale. Texture differs in three ways:
we start with the K-12 core curriculum, where open options are thinnest;
every book is a living, version-controlled project with a public repair
loop rather than a periodically revised PDF; and we're building txt, open
infrastructure any open-book project can use — including existing OER,
which we plan to support importing.

**A teacher found an error. What happens?**
Every page of every digital edition has a "report an issue" link (no
account needed). Reports become public issues; fixes are reviewed and land
in the next build — typically days, not years. Print owners can see
everything that changed since their printing.

**Can teachers adapt the books?**
Yes, legally and technically. CC BY 4.0 allows adaptation, translation,
and redistribution — even commercially — with attribution. txt makes it
practical: fork the book's source, change what you need, rebuild.
Adaptations can't use the Texture name as their brand (that guarantee is
reserved for editions that passed our review), but they can and should say
what they're based on.

**Are the books aligned to state standards?**
K-12 books carry machine-readable standards alignment (Common Core, NGSS,
and state frameworks) in their source, so alignment claims are verifiable
line by line — adoption committees can see exactly which sections address
which standards, rather than trusting a publisher's correlation chart.

**How do you handle politically contested topics?**
An independent Editorial Board of educators and scholars — not our
directors, not our donors — has sole authority over content, following
scholarly consensus and published, citable reasoning. Anyone may file a
content challenge; decisions are published. There is no private channel to
change a book, at any price.

**How does Texture sustain itself?**
Philanthropy (foundations and individuals), plus modest margins on print
editions that funders don't subsidize. Our costs fall as the toolchain
matures; commercial publishers' costs don't. We publish our finances —
Form 990s and annual reports — on the website.

**What about student privacy?**
Readers need no account; we collect no student data. The platform is for
adult contributors. This is a deliberate architectural choice, not a
policy promise.

**When can my school use a Texture book?**
The first pilot title (Algebra I) enters classrooms during Phase 2 of the
[roadmap](../roadmap.md). Districts interested in piloting can contact us
now — pilot feedback shapes the books.

**Can I help?**
Yes: educators and experts can join as contributors or reviewers
([requirements](../community/collaborators.md)), engineers can build txt,
and funders can create the runway. All of it happens in the open.
