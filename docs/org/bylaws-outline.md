# Texture — Bylaws Outline

> Skeleton for counsel to complete under the incorporation state's nonprofit
> statute. Drafted language is provided only where the wording is
> mission-critical (purpose, IRS clauses). **Not legal advice.**

Working corporate name: **Texture Open Textbooks, Inc.** (d/b/a "Texture")

---

## Article I — Name & Offices
Name, principal office, registered agent.

## Article II — Purpose

Draft purpose clause (also for the Articles of Incorporation):

> The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational
> purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue
> Code, including: (a) creating, publishing, and distributing openly licensed
> educational materials, including textbooks and curricula, free of charge in
> digital form and at minimal cost in print; (b) developing and maintaining
> free and open-source software that enables collaborative authoring,
> review, and publication of educational materials; and (c) supporting a
> community of educators, scholars, and contributors engaged in the creation
> and improvement of such materials.

## Article III — Membership
Recommend a **non-member corporation** (board-governed). Community voice is
provided through editorial governance
([../community/governance.md](../community/governance.md)), not corporate
membership — simpler legally, standard for this kind of org.

## Article IV — Board of Directors
- Size: 3–11 (start 5), majority independent.
- Terms: 3 years, staggered; term limits recommended (2 consecutive).
- Duties, elections, removal, vacancies.
- Meetings: minimum quarterly; notice; quorum (majority); action by
  unanimous written consent; remote participation allowed.
- No director compensation for board service (expense reimbursement OK).

## Article V — Officers
President/Chair, Secretary, Treasurer (Executive Director as non-officer
staff, hired by and reporting to the board). Duties, terms, removal.

## Article VI — Committees
- **Executive Committee** (optional, small boards can skip).
- **Finance & Audit Committee.**
- **Editorial Board** — chartered by the board but operating under the
  editorial governance policy; the corporate board sets scope and budget,
  not content decisions. This separation (board ≠ editorial control) is
  deliberate: it protects content neutrality and mirrors
  press/university-publisher practice.

## Article VII — Conflicts of Interest
Incorporate [conflict-of-interest-policy.md](conflict-of-interest-policy.md)
by reference; annual disclosure statements required.

## Article VIII — Indemnification
Standard indemnification of directors/officers to the extent permitted by
state law; authorize D&O insurance.

## Article IX — Fiscal Provisions
Fiscal year (recommend July 1–June 30, aligning with school years and most
education funders); books and records; annual budget; audit threshold;
public posting of Form 990.

## Article X — Amendments
Bylaws amendable by board supermajority (2/3) with advance notice.

## Appendix — IRS-required clauses (for the Articles)

**Private inurement / limitation of activities:**

> No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit
> of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, or other private
> persons, except that the corporation shall be authorized and empowered to
> pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and
> distributions in furtherance of the purposes set forth in Article II. No
> substantial part of the activities of the corporation shall be the carrying
> on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting to influence legislation, and the
> corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the
> publishing or distribution of statements) any political campaign on behalf
> of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.

**Dissolution:**

> Upon the dissolution of the corporation, assets shall be distributed for
> one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the
> Internal Revenue Code, or shall be distributed to the federal government,
> or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. In furtherance of
> the corporation's purposes, the board shall, to the extent practicable,
> ensure that the corporation's openly licensed educational materials and
> open-source software remain publicly available under their existing open
> licenses.

That last sentence is non-standard but important: it encodes the promise
that the books stay open even if the organization does not survive.
