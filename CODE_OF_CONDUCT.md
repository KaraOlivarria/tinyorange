# Texture — Code of Conduct

Version 0.1 (draft). Applies everywhere Texture work happens: the platform,
repositories, reviews, chat, events, and any space where you represent
Texture.

## Our standard

Texture is a professional community of educators, scholars, engineers, and
volunteers building materials for students. We hold ourselves to the
standard of a good faculty room: rigorous disagreement about the work,
unconditional respect for the people.

**Expected:**

- Critique ideas and content, never people. "This proof skips a step" —
  not "you clearly don't understand proofs."
- Assume good faith, especially across the educator/engineer divide;
  explain jargon when asked, without condescension.
- Give credit accurately. Cite sources. Flag your own uncertainty.
- Respect review outcomes and escalation paths, even when you disagree.
- Remember that students may read public threads about their textbooks.

**Unacceptable:**

- Harassment, discrimination, or demeaning conduct of any kind, including
  on the basis of race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, gender,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, age, disability, or role/credentials.
- Personal attacks, insults, or sustained hostility in reviews or disputes.
- Publishing others' private information without consent.
- Sexualized language or imagery in any project space (we build K-12
  materials; this is a bright line).
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.
- Deliberate sabotage of content, including politically or commercially
  motivated manipulation (see the
  [conflict of interest policy](docs/org/conflict-of-interest-policy.md)).

## Enforcement

- **Reports** go to conduct@ <!-- TODO: real address at launch --> or to any
  maintainer or Lead Editor. Reports are handled confidentially; reporters
  are told the outcome.
- **Responses** scale with severity and pattern: private warning, removal
  of content, temporary suspension, permanent removal from editorial roles
  or the platform. Lead Editors and maintainers act first; the Editorial
  Board hears appeals and handles reports about Lead Editors, maintainers,
  or board members themselves.
- Conduct decisions affecting editorial roles are recorded (privately) so
  patterns survive personnel changes.
