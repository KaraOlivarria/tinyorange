# Texture — Mission & Vision

## Mission Statement

> **Texture makes world-class textbooks a public good.**
>
> We bring leading educators together to create open-source, openly licensed
> textbooks for every classroom — beginning with the K-12 core curriculum and
> expanding into higher education. Every Texture textbook is free in digital
> form and available in print at the lowest sustainable cost. To make this
> collaboration possible, we build **txt**, an open, git-based typesetting
> platform where educators author, review, and publish together.

### The short form (for the website header, grant one-liners)

> Open textbooks, written by the world's best educators, free for every student.

### The one-paragraph form (for grant applications, press)

> Texture is a nonprofit organization building the next generation of
> educational textbooks. Textbook costs burden families, schools, and students,
> while the closed publishing model locks great teaching behind paywalls and
> slows the pace of improvement. Texture applies the proven model of
> open-source software to educational publishing: leading educators collaborate
> in the open on the Texture platform — powered by **txt**, our git-based
> collaborative typesetting tool — to produce rigorous, continuously improved,
> openly licensed textbooks. Digital editions are free forever; print editions
> are sold at the lowest sustainable cost. We begin with the K-12 core
> curriculum and will expand to higher education.

## Vision

A world where no student's education is limited by the price of a book, and
where the best explanations, examples, and exercises — from any educator,
anywhere — reach every classroom.

## The Problem

1. **Cost.** Textbook prices have far outpaced inflation. Districts ration
   adoptions; families and college students absorb the rest.
2. **Staleness.** Closed publishing cycles mean errors persist for years and
   content lags the state of knowledge and pedagogy.
3. **Lock-in.** Proprietary formats and licensing prevent teachers from
   adapting materials to their students.
4. **Wasted expertise.** Thousands of brilliant educators improve their own
   materials in isolation. Their work never compounds.

## The Model (Theory of Change)

Open-source software solved an analogous problem: expensive, closed, slowly
improving artifacts became free, open, and rapidly improving when the tooling
(version control, code review, distributed collaboration) made mass
collaboration practical. **Texture ports that toolchain to textbooks.**

- **txt** gives educators the equivalent of git + code review + CI for books:
  versioned source, structured review, automated typesetting to web, PDF,
  EPUB, and print.
- **The Texture platform** lowers the barrier so educators collaborate without
  learning git's internals.
- **Editorial governance** (subject-matter review + pedagogy review) keeps
  quality at or above commercial standards.
- **Open licensing** (CC BY 4.0) makes every book free to use, adapt,
  translate, and redistribute — forever.
- **Print-at-cost** partnerships put physical books in classrooms at a
  fraction of commercial prices, with any margin funding the mission.

## Values

1. **Access is the point.** Free digital, at-cost print, no exceptions.
2. **Rigor over speed.** Every published edition meets professional editorial
   standards. Open does not mean unvetted.
3. **Educators lead.** Content decisions are made by credentialed educators
   and subject-matter experts through transparent governance.
4. **Open by default.** Content, code, decisions, and finances are public
   unless there is a specific reason otherwise.
5. **Built to be adapted.** Every book is source-available and forkable —
   for translation, localization, accessibility, and classroom-level tailoring.
6. **Accessibility is a requirement, not a feature.** Every edition ships
   meeting WCAG 2.2 AA or better.
7. **Neutral and evidence-based.** Content follows scholarly consensus and
   established curriculum standards; the organization is non-partisan.

## Scope & Sequencing

| Phase | Focus | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Organization + tooling | Incorporate, seed board, build txt to a usable v1, publish format spec |
| 2 | K-12 core curriculum pilots | 2–3 subjects (e.g., Algebra I, Biology, U.S. History), full editorial pipeline |
| 3 | K-12 core curriculum breadth | Math, science, ELA, social studies across grade bands; standards alignment |
| 4 | Higher education | Introductory college courses (highest-cost commercial titles first) |
| Ongoing | Translation & localization | Community-driven, editorially reviewed |

## What Success Looks Like

- A district can adopt a complete, standards-aligned Texture curriculum at
  zero digital cost.
- A teacher can file an erratum and see it fixed in the next build, not the
  next decade.
- A researcher can trace every claim in a book to its review history.
- A community can translate a book without asking permission.
